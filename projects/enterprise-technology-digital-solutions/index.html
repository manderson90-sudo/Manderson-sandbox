PROJECT OVERVIEW

Building a technology function from the ground up

I joined Newcastle United Football Club in May 2023 as the first employee in a newly established IT Applications & Digital Development function.

Over the following three years, I built and developed the team, expanded its responsibilities across more than 200 applications and established stronger relationships between technology and departments throughout the organisation.

What began as a function focused on centrally managed applications, employee support and technology adoption developed into a team responsible for enterprise application governance, technology business partnering and digital solutions.

As the function evolved, I developed and secured approval for its rebrand to Enterprise Technology & Digital Solutions, reflecting its wider responsibilities and contribution to the Club.

One of the outcomes I'm most proud of was the change in how technology was perceived across the organisation.

Through closer collaboration, improved governance and practical technology solutions, we helped develop a more proactive approach to supporting departments and identifying opportunities for continuous improvement.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Supporting a growing organisation with an increasingly complex technology environment

When I joined Newcastle United, the newly established function was primarily intended to manage and improve centrally supported IT applications.

The original responsibilities included application support, employee training, instructional guidance, troubleshooting and the implementation of new technology.

As I developed a better understanding of the Club's wider technology environment, it became clear that the scale of application usage extended considerably beyond the systems managed directly by IT.

Departments were independently purchasing and managing software, particularly cloud-based SaaS applications.

This created challenges around application ownership, supplier visibility, contractual commitments, security, documentation and technology governance.

The Club's continued growth also increased the importance of having technology specialists who understood the requirements of different departments.

I recognised an opportunity to develop the function into a more proactive technology partner, helping departments make informed decisions and improve their processes.

02 / ESTABLISHING THE FUNCTION

From the first employee to an established team

I joined the function as its first employee, with responsibility for establishing how it would operate and contribute to the wider Technology department.

As the scope of the function became clearer, I identified the need for additional resources to support the Club's growing application landscape and departmental requirements.

I developed business cases to recruit three IT Applications Analysts, building the initial team structure and introducing greater capacity to support departments across the Club.

Recruitment was an important part of developing the function, alongside establishing clear responsibilities, effective working relationships and a shared approach to service improvement.

I wanted colleagues to understand the operational challenges facing the departments they supported and feel confident identifying opportunities for technology to make a difference.

FUNCTION DEVELOPMENT

Building the team from its first employee

Established the function and recruited three IT Applications Analysts, creating a four-person team with the capacity to develop closer relationships across the organisation.

03 / ENTERPRISE APPLICATION MANAGEMENT

Establishing visibility across more than 200 applications

One of the most significant developments came as I identified the scale of technology operating outside the central IT function.

The discovery of widespread Shadow IT highlighted the need for stronger application governance and more consistent technology oversight.

I led work to establish a centralised Applications Portfolio covering more than 200 systems used across the Club.

This provided greater visibility of application ownership, suppliers, contractual commitments, technology risks and opportunities for improvement.

The work also expanded the function's responsibilities beyond traditional software administration.

Our oversight increasingly involved business-critical technology across Football, Commercial and Operations, alongside systems connected to physical infrastructure, including access control and stadium LED technology.

I worked with senior stakeholders to communicate identified risks and develop a more structured approach to managing the Club's technology environment.

04 / TECHNOLOGY GOVERNANCE

Introducing stronger application governance and lifecycle management

As the application portfolio developed, I introduced improvements to how technology was assessed, documented and managed throughout its lifecycle.

This included strengthening application ownership, supplier management, procurement oversight and the documentation available to the Technology department.

A particular focus was improving visibility of applications purchased independently by departments and encouraging earlier engagement with IT when considering new technology.

I also worked to improve identity and access management, including the adoption of Microsoft Entra ID and Single Sign-On across the application estate.

Alongside security improvements, the function supported more informed decisions about application selection, ongoing value, renewal and retirement.

Application Portfolio

Improved visibility of application ownership, suppliers, contracts and technology risks.

Procurement Governance

Encouraged earlier Technology involvement in application selection and purchasing decisions.

Lifecycle Management

Strengthened oversight of application onboarding, ongoing management, optimisation and retirement.

Security & Identity

Supported stronger access controls and consistent identity management across enterprise applications.

These improvements formed part of a wider effort to establish a more consistent and accountable approach to technology management across Newcastle United.

05 / TECHNOLOGY BUSINESS PARTNERING

Building closer relationships across Football, Commercial and Operations

A central part of the function's development was moving towards a proactive technology business partnering model.

Rather than waiting for departments to raise technical issues, I wanted the team to understand their priorities, challenges and opportunities for improvement.

The three analysts developed dedicated relationships across the Club's main business areas.

Football

Supporting technology requirements across football operations, including performance, recruitment, analysis, medical and player development functions.

Commercial

Working with departments including ticketing, retail, hospitality, partnerships, marketing and commercial operations.

Operations

Supporting business functions including venue operations, safety, people, finance and organisational services.

Collectively, these business areas included relationships with 66 Heads of Department.

The analysts increasingly supported technology selection, procurement, application onboarding, process improvement and opportunities for automation.

I developed a proposal to formally recognise these responsibilities through the title Technology Business Partner.

The model strengthened the connection between Technology and the wider organisation, helping departments access advice and support from colleagues who understood their operational requirements.

08 / EVOLVING THE FUNCTION

Establishing Enterprise Technology & Digital Solutions

By January 2026, the function's responsibilities had developed considerably beyond those originally established in May 2023.

Its work now included enterprise application management, technology governance, business partnering, supplier management and digital transformation.

I prepared a formal proposal outlining the function's development and the organisational changes needed to support its future.

A central recommendation was to rebrand the team as Enterprise Technology & Digital Solutions.

I secured approval for the rebrand, establishing a name that better reflected the function's enterprise-wide responsibilities and strategic contribution.

The proposal also outlined the development of dedicated Technology Business Partner roles and opportunities to strengthen internal digital development capabilities.

Enterprise Technology

Reflecting the function's responsibility for organisation-wide applications, governance, integrations, security and technology management.

Digital Solutions

Emphasising the identification and delivery of practical technology solutions, process improvements, automation and digital transformation.

Technology Business Partners

Recognising the existing departmental relationships and technology advisory responsibilities developed by the three analysts.

Specialist Development Capabilities

Developed business cases for Power Platform and SharePoint specialist roles to increase internal delivery capability and reduce reliance on external technology platforms.

This work represented the next stage in developing a more mature and strategically aligned technology function.
