Identity & Access Management.

Modernising application access across the Club

01 / THE CHALLENGE

A fragmented application landscape

When I joined Newcastle United in May 2023, only one of the Club's SaaS applications had been integrated with single sign-on (SSO).

Departments across the Club were purchasing and implementing their own software. Teams in areas such as Football Data & Insights, HR and Marketing had their own requirements and budgets, with applications often selected, purchased and configured without IT involvement.

This had created a large and fragmented application landscape. Employees were managing separate credentials across multiple systems, while IT had limited oversight of how applications were configured and how access was managed.

I recognised an opportunity to improve security, simplify access for colleagues and establish greater oversight of the technology being used across the business.

02 / BENCHMARKING

Learning from other Premier League clubs

One of the first things I did was speak to people in similar roles at other Premier League clubs.

Through my professional network, I benchmarked our approach to application management against clubs including Manchester City, Arsenal and Chelsea.

These conversations helped me understand how other organisations were managing their SaaS environments.

Manchester City was a particularly useful example. Their IT function had been embedded in application procurement for several years, with clear technical requirements established before new platforms were purchased.

Support for single sign-on was one of those requirements.

It became apparent that Newcastle United had considerable ground to make up.

I used what I'd learned to develop a proposal for improving our approach, initially focusing on integrating the Club's existing critical and high-risk applications with Microsoft Entra ID.

03 / BUSINESS CASE

Securing investment for a rapid onboarding programme

Addressing the existing application landscape required additional investment.

There were too many applications to integrate quickly using our internal resources alone, particularly alongside the team's existing operational responsibilities.

I developed a business case proposing a six-month onboarding programme, supported by a trusted managed service provider.

The aim was to accelerate the integration of critical and high-risk applications while establishing the processes and internal knowledge needed to manage future integrations ourselves.

I presented the proposal to the Club's executive leadership team and secured approval for the investment.

From there, I took responsibility for the project, including the budget, delivery plan, milestones, supplier management and coordination of stakeholders across the Club.

04 / DELIVERY

Integrating more than 80 applications

Over the following six months, we successfully integrated more than 80 applications with Microsoft Entra ID.

Each application presented its own challenges.

Some already supported SSO and required relatively straightforward configuration. Others needed upgrades to enterprise subscription plans before the functionality became available.

I worked with application vendors, account managers, budget holders and internal stakeholders to assess the requirements and coordinate each integration.

This involved managing the commercial implications of subscription upgrades, agreeing implementation arrangements and ensuring changes followed the Club's established change management processes.

The technical integrations were delivered through a combination of our managed service provider and internal resources, with me overseeing the programme and coordinating delivery.

05 / MANAGING RISK

Protecting business-critical matchday operations

One of the most challenging integrations involved the Club's ticketing platform.

This was a particularly sensitive system because of its importance to matchday operations and the Club's revenue.

The platform supported ticketing and stadium access for more than 50,000 supporters, so any disruption could have had serious operational consequences.

I personally managed this integration from planning through to implementation.

Rather than attempting to migrate everyone at once, I developed a phased approach that allowed us to transition individual departments in a controlled way.

We carefully scheduled changes around the football calendar, respecting matchday change freezes and avoiding periods when the system was needed for critical operations.

Each stage was planned and coordinated to minimise disruption and allow us to manage any issues before progressing.

0 Matchday disruptions caused by the ticketing platform SSO migration

The migration was completed successfully without affecting matchday operations.

For me, this was one of the most satisfying parts of the project. It demonstrated that significant changes to business-critical technology could be delivered safely with the right planning and stakeholder involvement.

06 / EMPLOYEE ADOPTION

Helping colleagues adapt

Introducing SSO also meant changing how colleagues accessed systems they used every day.

For some employees, this was their first experience of signing into applications using their organisational Microsoft account.

Applications such as the Club's intranet and HR platforms were used widely across the business, including on mobile devices.

We needed to make sure colleagues understood what was changing and how to access their systems following the migration.

01

Drop-in sessions

Organised practical support sessions where colleagues could ask questions and receive help with the new sign-in process.

02

Lunch-and-learn events

Helped employees understand what SSO meant, how it worked and what would change when accessing their applications.

03

Mandatory learning

Introduced learning content to explain SSO and improve colleagues' understanding of secure application access.

07 / APPLICATION GOVERNANCE

Improving application documentation

The programme also gave us an opportunity to improve the information IT held about individual applications.

As systems were onboarded, we created Service in Live articles within our knowledge base.

These documented important information about each application's ownership, support arrangements and commercial commitments.

01

Business accountability

Documented business owners, budget holders and IT responsibilities for each application.

02

Supplier information

Recorded supplier contacts, account managers and support arrangements.

03

Commercial visibility

Captured contract lengths, application costs and renewal dates to support supplier management and future cost-saving initiatives.

This provided a clearer understanding of who was responsible for each application and how it was managed.

The information also supported wider initiatives covering application governance, supplier management and opportunities to reduce unnecessary expenditure.

08 / LONG-TERM CHANGE

Making SSO part of everyday operations

The initial six-month programme was designed to address the existing backlog of applications.

However, new software continued to be purchased across the Club, so it was important that the improvements continued after the project finished.

Following the programme, my team became embedded in the application procurement process.

We established SSO support as a minimum requirement when evaluating new applications and ensured IT was involved before platforms were purchased and implemented.

We also developed the capability to deliver new SSO integrations internally.

My team completed most of these integrations, and I continued to configure several applications myself.

What began as a project to address an existing problem became part of how the Club managed technology on an ongoing basis.

09 / THE OUTCOME

A more consistent approach to application access

80+ Applications integrated with Microsoft Entra ID in six months

The programme significantly improved how Newcastle United managed access to its SaaS applications.

Moving more than 80 applications onto Microsoft Entra ID reduced the number of separate credentials colleagues needed to manage and made it easier to access systems through a central application launcher.

We also saw fewer password-related support tickets, improved access controls and simpler processes for managing access when colleagues joined or left the organisation.

The project strengthened IT's involvement in application procurement and helped establish clearer ownership and documentation across the application landscape.

By embedding SSO requirements into procurement and developing the team's internal capability, we established a more consistent approach that continued long after the initial project had finished.
