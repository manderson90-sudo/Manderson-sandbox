PROJECT OVERVIEW

Improving commercial control across the club's technology landscape

During my time at Newcastle United, I led a programme of technology supplier rationalisation and cost optimisation that reduced annual recurring contract costs by £225,000.

The programme involved reviewing applications purchased across the organisation, identifying duplicated functionality, consolidating suppliers and challenging whether existing software represented the best solution for the club.

Alongside the financial savings, I established a centralised Applications Portfolio using Monday.com, introducing greater visibility of technology expenditure and automated contract renewal management.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

A fragmented approach to technology purchasing

When I joined Newcastle United in May 2023, technology purchasing was largely decentralised.

Individual departments were independently procuring and managing software applications, particularly SaaS platforms, often without involving IT in the purchasing process.

Although IT maintained records of its own suppliers and contracts, there was no complete centralised view of technology expenditure across the wider organisation.

This created several challenges, including duplicated functionality, limited visibility of contractual commitments and missed opportunities to negotiate more favourable commercial arrangements.

Contract renewals presented a particular problem. Departments were sometimes unaware of approaching notice periods or began reviewing their options too late.

Without sufficient time to assess alternatives or plan a migration, departments could find themselves committed to another contract period for software that no longer represented the best option.

02 / ESTABLISHING VISIBILITY

Building a centralised Applications Portfolio

I worked with departments across the club to establish a clearer understanding of the applications they were using, the suppliers involved and the associated contractual commitments.

Using Monday.com, I introduced a centralised Applications Portfolio to bring this information together.

The portfolio provided a structured record of application ownership, budget responsibility, suppliers, contract costs, renewal dates and contractual notice periods.

A key part of the implementation was the introduction of automated notifications linked to upcoming notice periods.

These reminders gave the team sufficient time to review an application's ongoing value, investigate alternatives, negotiate with suppliers or coordinate a migration before a renewal deadline.

03 / SUPPLIER CONSOLIDATION

Identifying duplication across departments

As the application landscape became clearer, I identified several areas where different departments were purchasing software with overlapping functionality.

One example was digital signage.

The club was operating three separate digital signage platforms across the training ground, hospitality facilities and retail operations.

I worked with the relevant stakeholders to consolidate these arrangements onto a single platform, reducing supplier duplication and simplifying ongoing management.

A similar opportunity existed with project management software.

Three different project management platforms were being used across the organisation.

By reviewing departmental requirements and identifying overlapping functionality, I coordinated the move to one retained platform and the exit of the other suppliers at appropriate renewal points.

CONSOLIDATION OUTCOMES

Fewer platforms. Greater consistency.

Both digital signage and project management were consolidated from three separate platforms to one, reducing unnecessary contracts and simplifying the club's application landscape.

04 / HOSPITALITY OPERATIONS

Replacing unsuitable SaaS software with Microsoft Power Apps

Supplier rationalisation also involved challenging whether existing applications were appropriate for the club's operational requirements.

One example involved a SaaS platform purchased by the Sales Development department to manage table arrangements within premium hospitality suites.

The application was primarily designed for restaurants and did not adequately accommodate the complexity of managing hospitality arrangements within a football stadium.

I identified an opportunity for my team to develop a more suitable solution using Microsoft Power Apps.

The resulting application was tailored to the department's requirements and could be supported internally during matchdays.

This provided the department with a better-suited operational solution while allowing the club to discontinue the existing SaaS contract.

PRACTICAL IMPROVEMENT

A solution designed around stadium operations

The Power Apps replacement provided functionality tailored to the department's requirements, with internal support available during business-critical matchday operations.

05 / MARKETING & CREATIVE

Making better use of the club's existing project management platform

Another opportunity emerged within the Marketing and Creative function.

The department had purchased a standalone SaaS application to manage internal marketing and creative design requests.

Following the consolidation of the club's project management platforms, I identified an opportunity to accommodate these requirements within the retained system.

My team developed an internal request management workflow using the existing project management platform.

This allowed the department to continue managing incoming requests without maintaining a separate SaaS subscription.

The change reduced unnecessary expenditure and demonstrated the value of reviewing existing technology capabilities before purchasing additional applications.

06 / COMMERCIAL LEADERSHIP

Leading supplier negotiations and procurement decisions

I took the lead on supplier negotiations, procurement decisions and contract rationalisation throughout the programme.

Working closely with Finance and Legal, I reviewed contractual commitments, assessed commercial opportunities and coordinated decisions with departmental stakeholders.

The Applications Portfolio provided greater visibility of existing commitments and helped identify upcoming opportunities to renegotiate, consolidate or exit contracts.

This supported a more proactive approach to commercial management, with decisions informed by application usage, departmental requirements, available alternatives and contractual deadlines.

07 / FINANCIAL OUTCOMES

Reducing annual recurring contract costs by £225,000

The programme delivered a £225,000 reduction in annual recurring technology contract costs during my time at Newcastle United.

This represented the combined annual contract value removed through the cancellation of unnecessary subscriptions, supplier consolidation and replacement of selected SaaS applications with internally supported alternatives.

Alongside the financial savings, the work reduced application duplication and improved visibility of technology expenditure across the club.

KEY ACHIEVEMENT

£225,000 annual reduction in recurring contract costs

Achieved through supplier rationalisation, contract cancellation, platform consolidation and better use of existing technology.

08 / LONG-TERM IMPROVEMENT

Establishing a more proactive approach to supplier governance

The programme also established a more structured approach to managing the club's technology investments.

The centralised Applications Portfolio brought together supplier information, application ownership, contractual commitments and renewal dates.

Automated notifications helped ensure the team had sufficient time to evaluate applications before contractual notice periods expired.

This improved the club's ability to make informed procurement decisions, assess supplier value and plan application changes in advance.

The approach also supported the wider application governance improvements I was introducing across Newcastle United.
